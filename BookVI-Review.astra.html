The judgment

Insight is not a certificate of authority.

Book VI contains useful cautions, a revealing memoir and an ambitious research agenda. It does not establish that its author, readers or preferred institutions possess a reliable general method for superior judgment. At crucial moments, autobiographical conviction and attractive explanations do work that measurements should be doing.

The strongest objections are concrete. An FDA estimate changes units. A psychotherapy experiment changes its comparison group in the retelling. Important psychology claims lose force under replication. Elsewhere, claims about religion, ancestral psychology, cooperation and personal improvement outrun the evidence offered. Bayesian mathematics cannot fill those gaps.

A hard factual error

The FDA estimate gains an extra zero.

Raised in Technophilia turns decade-based estimates into annual figures. The roughly tenfold inflation is a checkable reporting error, independent of the policy debate.

Gieringer (1985), printed p. 196 · Read the annotations

The deeper failure

A recurring shift in evidential standards.

Other schools are asked to prove their methods. The proposed rationality movement is often carried by memoir, analogy and hope. That is enough to propose experiments. It is insufficient to earn deference.

The fundraising chapters expose a further tension. Readers are urged to account for opportunity cost, yet refusal to join or fund favored projects can become evidence of defective cooperation. An informed donor can reasonably reject the project. The relevant standard is comparative expected benefit, not whether the organizer feels that the community has rallied.

A qualification that changes the verdict

The book repeatedly admits that the art is incomplete, discloses failures and invites readers to improve or replace it. It would be a straw man to claim that every essay announces a scientifically validated curriculum. The objection is to the surrounding overconfidence and unsupported prescriptions—not to the legitimacy of proposing a research agenda.

What the studies actually permit

Failure, uncertainty and overreach are different findings.

A failed replication lowers confidence in the effect tested. A different population or outcome may limit generalization without refuting the original experiment. An untested prescription remains unestablished even when its underlying psychological phenomenon is real.

This is a targeted audit of consequential examples, not a claim that every study in the book failed or that every relevant later paper has been located. The annotations identify whether a source directly tests the claim, limits its scope, or supplies related evidence.

The logical problems are larger than the replication problems.

Bayes does not select the model.

Consistency rules do not supply correct priors, hypotheses, causal structure or values. The boy–girl puzzle illustrates how a reporting protocol can change a mathematically valid answer.

A story does not establish a method.

Self-correction may be real without showing which practice caused it, whether readers can reproduce it or whether it outperforms alternatives.

Possible does not mean worth pursuing.

Logical possibility, catastrophic stakes and intense commitment do not supply tractability estimates, opportunity costs or stopping rules.

Further recurring shortcuts include inferring an evolutionary history from a plausible function, treating the same action after new evidence as proof of a failed update, and presenting psychological separability as an optimization theorem. These criticisms are arguments about what follows from the stated premises. They are not experimental findings dressed up as a new authority.

What remains unscathed?

The discipline of correction, with its scope kept honest.

Several principles emerge intact in their limited form. No whole empirical package emerges with a clean bill of health.

  • Bayes’s theorem and explicit assumptions.

    The mathematics is unaffected by these criticisms. It remains necessary to justify the model and distinguish mathematical consistency from practical competence.

  • Winning an argument is not knowing the truth.

    An opponent’s bad reasoning cannot establish your conclusion. The author correctly applies this to his younger self; this review must follow the same rule.

  • Evaluate methods separately from reputations.

    Tests of individual performance, instructional methods and institutional assessment answer different questions. The distinction in Three Levels remains particularly useful.

  • Rationality groups can become dogmatic.

    Epistemic Viciousness explicitly applies its warning to rationality schools. Scientific language and a shared identity supply no immunity.

  • Respect refusal and individual variation.

    Beware of Other-Optimizing retains a strong practical core. Its case for accepting “no,” especially under unequal power, does not depend on one priming experiment.

  • Some reasoning skills can be improved.

    Statistical training, targeted debiasing and scored forecasting provide bounded examples. They support further experiments, not a blanket claim of exceptional rational ability.

  • Intelligence does not guarantee benevolence.

    The retracted inference was invalid. The correction leaves open the probabilities, capabilities and institutional choices involved in any particular AI-risk claim.

  • Effort, opportunity cost and permission to stop.

    Concrete attempts can teach; specialization can help; failure is possible. These remain sound when the costs, alternatives and stopping conditions are explicit.

The strongest individual essays are Three Levels of Rationality Verification, Epistemic Viciousness and the central warning in Beware of Other-Optimizing. Their empirical illustrations and universal language still need qualifications. The ending’s instruction to read beyond one author is also worth following literally.

What would earn stronger claims?

A specified curriculum, suitable comparison groups, preregistered outcomes, meaningful transfer tests, follow-up, costs and attrition, and independent replication. Agreement with the teacher, memorable success stories and fundraising totals cannot stand in for those results.

Moore and Healy distinguish estimates of one’s performance, placement relative to others and precision of belief. A person can be too pessimistic on one dimension while being too certain on another. time out of three

Trying a difficult task can reveal information that introspection misses. Sources and limits of inference.

Original papers and author/publisher records are linked. Years in parentheses distinguish online and issue dates where useful. Effect estimates from different tasks and designs should not be treated as interchangeable.

  1. Fong, Krantz & Nisbett (1986). The effects of statistical training on thinking about everyday problems

    Experiments on statistical reasoning and transfer; not a test of this book or of overall life success.

  2. de Heide & Grünwald (2021). Why optional stopping can be a problem for Bayesians

    Distinguishes Bayesian stopping-rule independence from calibration under different uses of priors.

  3. Sisk et al. (2018). To what extent and under which circumstances are growth mind-sets important to academic achievement? Two meta-analyses

    Small average associations/intervention effects with moderators; not a direct replication of every praise experiment.

  4. Carpenter, Zucker & Avorn (2008). Drug-review deadlines and safety problems

    Observational association between deadline-proximate approvals and safety problems; does not estimate the net mortality effect of regulation.

  5. Bennett (1973). Logical reversibility of computation

    Primary theoretical paper on reversible computation and dissipation.

  6. Halpern (1999). A counterexample to theorems of Cox and Fine

    Limits particular theorem formulations and assumptions; does not refute probability theory.

  7. Gardner (1970). Mathematical Games: The fantastic combinations of John Conway’s new solitaire game “life”

    Reproduction of the original Scientific American description, including births at three neighbors.

  8. Maimonides, Repentance 2:2. Mishneh Torah, Hilchot Teshuvah

    Primary religious text specifying abandonment of wrongdoing and resolve about future conduct.

  9. Gollwitzer & Sheeran (2006). Implementation intentions and goal achievement: A meta-analysis of effects and processes

    Evidence about concrete if–then planning, not the efficacy of exhortations to try harder.

  10. Sanchez et al. (2017). Direct replication of Gervais & Norenzayan (2012): No evidence that analytic thinking decreases religious belief

    Direct replication of one acute picture-prime experiment; later related evidence, not a study cited by the essay or a test of multiyear education.

  11. Mihura et al. (2013). The validity of individual Rorschach variables: Systematic reviews and meta-analyses of the Comprehensive System

    Variable-specific validity evidence, with major limitations; does not validate unrestricted inkblot interpretation.

  12. Strupp & Hadley (1979). Specific vs nonspecific factors in psychotherapy: A controlled study of outcome

    Small, selected male college sample and professors chosen for interpersonal ability; not random professors or all psychiatric conditions.

  13. Cuijpers et al. (2023). Cognitive behavior therapy vs. control conditions, other psychotherapies, pharmacotherapies and combined treatment for depression

    409 trials and 52,702 patients; meaningful benefits vs controls, little robust superiority over other psychotherapies.

  14. Morewedge et al. (2015). Debiasing decisions: Improved decision making with a single training intervention

    Experimental improvement on measured biases; bounded evidence of trainability.

  15. Sellier, Scopelliti & Morewedge (2019). Debiasing training improves decision making in the field

    Transfer to a business decision case in graduate training; narrower than general life competence.

  16. Mellers et al. (2014). Psychological strategies for winning a geopolitical forecasting tournament

    Probability training, teamwork and aggregation in scored geopolitical forecasts; not a test of the Sequences curriculum.

  17. Cameron (1999). Raising the stakes in the ultimatum game: Experimental evidence from Indonesia

    Some large offers rejected, alongside greater willingness to accept a given share at higher stakes.

  18. Hill et al. (2011). Co-residence patterns in hunter-gatherer societies show unique human social structure

    32 contemporary foraging societies; indirect evidence about ancestry, with mobility and broad social networks.

  19. Iannaccone (1992). Sacrifice and stigma: Reducing free-riding in cults, communes, and other collectives

    A club-goods model in which costly requirements can support cooperation; not a universal empirical vindication of religion.

    23 laboratories; N=2,141; d=0.04, 95% CI [−0.07, 0.15].

    36 laboratories; N=3,531; nonsignificant confirmatory d=0.06; exploratory analyses differed.

    12 laboratories; N=1,775; small significant d=0.10, rising to 0.16 after a response-quality exclusion.

    105 independent effect sizes, over 7,700 participants; average individual-level inhibition, moderated by situation.

    219 observed conflicts; at least one helper in roughly 9/10. Aggregate outcome, not a direct replication of laboratory individual inhibition.

    Cold-water discomfort experiment; not a demonstration that smaller meals produce identical memories.

    Distinguishes overestimation, overplacement and overprecision; broad labels can hide different directions of error.

    Exam-answer changes and remembered regret; evidence against a blanket first-answer rule, not against individual measurement.
